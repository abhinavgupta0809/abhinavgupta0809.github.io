Teardowns

A teardown takes one problem from a dashboard and follows it through to a working fix.

Live

Perplexity: what did that run cost me?

  1. The signal

    In the Perplexity dashboard, money leads the complaints, and 202 reviews come from paying users who ran out of credits. 99 of those are on iOS.

  2. The problem

    Paying users who can't predict their credits. I measured it on my own agent runs: what each one cost, where it ran, and whether its numbers checked out.

  3. The fix

    Run Receipt: one receipt, shown at three moments of a run, starting with a cost range before you send. The site also sets out what I'd measure and at what bar I'd hold the rollout.

Limits: these are my own runs, not user interviews, so how much this matters to Perplexity users is still a hypothesis. The prototype replays recorded runs and doesn't hook into Perplexity's product.

Case study

Cent Capital internship · Summer 2026

VaultLedger: the finding was the failed experiments

VaultLedger answers plain-English questions about financial documents such as bank statements, invoices and pay stubs. It runs fully on the device, quotes its source for every answer, and refuses when it can't find support. With someone's taxes, a confidently wrong number is worse than no answer.

0.50 to 0.79
MRR after hybrid retrieval plus a cross-encoder reranker, on an 80-question golden set
44% to 70%
Answer accuracy band: strict match at the low end, LLM judge at the high end
5 of 5
Preregistered experiments that missed their preset bar, including one I cut after it turned refusals into wrong answers

What I'd defend: the bottleneck was over-refusal, not search or model size. 19 of 70 answerable questions were refused, and in about 75% of those the right document was already retrieved. That's a decision-policy problem.

What it isn't: production-ready. The test set is self-authored, so 70% is a measured, reproducible number and not a claim of being good.

CivicSignal

Live

An AI credibility assistant for news articles.

Paste an article or analyze the current tab with the Chrome extension. You get a credibility score from 0 to 100, a breakdown across four signals, and 3 to 5 extracted claims marked Supported, Disputed or Unverified. RescueMesh

Hackathon prototype · synthetic data

A multi-agency command center simulation for a flash flood.

Five Gemini "chiefs" (incident command, medical, police, rescue and logistics) deliberate over a frozen snapshot of the world and return advice only. A human approves every plan. A deterministic TypeScript engine is the only thing that changes state, and it re-checks availability, double-booking and closed routes before applying anything.

Built at HackCMU, September 2026. Exercise only: all data is synthetic and it isn't for real dispatch.

VaultLedger

Internship build · not production-ready

A local-first assistant for financial documents.

Point it at a folder of PDFs and ask questions in plain English. It searches the documents with hybrid retrieval (embeddings plus keywords), answers from a local model through Ollama, quotes its source and refuses when it can't find support. Nothing leaves the machine. It ships with an eval suite and an 80-question benchmark.
